Artificial Intelligence in Social Media: Opportunities, Risks, and Governance
Literature-review dataset and thematic evidence map | 2024–2026

This repository accompanies the manuscript “Artificial Intelligence in Social Media: A Systematic Review of Opportunities, Risks, and Governance.” It provides a structured catalogue of journal articles examining the intersection of artificial intelligence (AI) and social media, with a focus on technological applications, associated societal risks, and governance responses.
Overview
The study uses a scoping-review approach, reported with reference to PRISMA-ScR, and applies an iterative qualitative synthesis informed by open and axial coding. The resulting conceptual framework connects three elements:
1. AI application domains in social media;
2. Cross-cutting societal risks, including misinformation, privacy erosion, algorithmic bias, limited transparency, and polarization;
3. Multi-layer governance responses spanning technological, regulatory, social, and ethical dimensions.
The review maps 268 included journal articles published during 2024–2026. Journal Q1 status was an eligibility filter, not an independent assessment of study quality.
Literature search and selection
The search was conducted on 5 June 2026 using Web of Science Core Collection and Scopus.
Database	Search query
Web of Science Core Collection	TS=("artificial intelligence" AND "social media")

The review applied database-specific restrictions relating to publication type, language, publication period, disciplinary/index coverage, and journal quartile. The search is limited to the two specified phrases and two databases; articles using alternative terminology may therefore be underrepresented.

Thematic coverage
The evidence map covers eight application domains:
1. Healthcare
2. Education
3. Work and professional life
4. Disinformation and misinformation
5. Deepfakes and synthetic content
6. Public policy and society
7. Communication and media studies
8. Algorithmic governance
The synthesis considers AI-enabled opportunities—such as personalization, early detection, and decision support—alongside social and ethical risks and potential governance measures.

How to use this repository
Download the Excel file and use its article titles, DOIs, thematic categories, and methodological fields to explore the literature. You can filter studies by application area, social media platform, AI method, or study methodology. For scholarly reuse, verify each source and its bibliographic details using the publisher's record or DOI landing page.

No analysis scripts or automated reproduction pipeline are included in the supplied materials. The manuscript describes manual/interpretive screening and thematic synthesis; the workbook alone does not reproduce every screening decision or the original database queries.
Limitations and data quality
- Coverage is restricted to the specified databases, queries, filtering criteria, and search date.
- Q1 status is a bibliometric selection criterion and must not be interpreted as proof of methodological rigor.
- The review did not perform a study-level critical appraisal, so the catalogue should not be used to infer comparative effectiveness or causal validity.
- Study summaries, citation mappings, DOI entries, and thematic classifications should be verified against the original publications before consequential reuse.
- The manuscript's selection-flow figure requires correction to match the written counts above.
